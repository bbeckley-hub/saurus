One Health, one action: Prevent AMR now.

The 2026 WHO campaign theme — and the reason SAURUS exists. Resistance moves between people, animals, food, and the environment. Surveillance has to move with it.

Source: WHO Global Antimicrobial Resistance and Use Surveillance System (GLASS) 2025 report

01 🌱
Emergence
Antimicrobial use — in medicine, agriculture, and food production — creates selection pressure. Susceptible bacteria die, resistant mutants survive, and the population shifts.
02 🔗
Spread
Resistant organisms move between humans, animals, and the environment. Poor infection prevention and control (IPC) accelerates every link in that chain.
03 ⚠️
Consequence
Bacteria can stack multiple resistance mechanisms, narrowing treatment options to nothing — while the pipeline of new antimicrobials stays thin.
04 🛡️
Response
WHO names the two levers: prudent antimicrobial use in every setting, and high standards of IPC. Surveillance is how we know where those levers are needed.

MRSA was described in 1961 — just two years after methicillin entered clinical use. Within a generation it had spread to every continent. USA300, the community-associated MRSA clone that emerged in the United States around the turn of the millennium, has since spread to every inhabited continent. Every lineage we track is a descendant of that story — and the lineages that will dominate the next generation are being written right now in hospitals whose genomes have not yet been sequenced.

Staphylococcus aureus is one of the eight bacterial pathogens tracked in the WHO GLASS 2025 report, and 27.1% of its bloodstream infections were methicillin-resistant in 2023, rising to 50.3% in the Eastern Mediterranean Region. GLASS tells us how much resistance exists; genome-level surveillance tells us which lineages carry it, and where they are moving.

SAURUS puts 134,160 genomes of surveillance in any researcher's browser. No install. No login. No cost. And it's built on the same principle WHO calls for this week: One Health, one action.

🚀 How to Use SAURUS

Four steps from query to genome download — no installation, no account, no limits.

1

Browse a module

Pick a typing scheme (MLST, spa, SCCmec…) or the new Geography page to explore metadata directly.

2

Combine genotype + geography

Stack filters: e.g. ST5 + CC5 + MRSA + United States + blood + mecA. Every module carries country and source columns alongside the typing data.

3

Export accessions

Click 🧬 Accessions to download a plain-text list of matching genome accession IDs — one per line.

4

Download genomes

Feed the list to NCBI: datasets download genome accession --inputfile list.txt — that's it.

📥 Raw Sequencing Reads

22,322 genomes (16.6%) have direct pointers to raw FASTQ files in the global SRA consortium (NCBI SRA, ENA, DDBJ). Export Run, Sample, or Project accessions from any typing page and download with fastq-dl or the NCBI SRA Toolkit. Coverage reflects what's actually deposited — many assembled genomes have no associated raw reads.

🧪 BioSamples (recommended)

One BioSample per matched genome. Precise — only SAURUS genomes. fastq-dl resolves each sample to its runs automatically.

📥 SRA Runs

One SRR/ERR/DRR per line. Most precise — exact run accessions. Some BioSamples have multiple runs (reference strains can have 10–100+).

📦 BioProjects (bulk)

One BioProject per matched project. May include non-S. aureus runs from mixed-species studies — verify before bulk download.

🌍 Assemblies: AllTheBacteria

Don't need FASTQs? AllTheBacteria provides uniformly processed assemblies — plus raw-read provenance and metadata — for millions of public bacterial genomes, including the bulk of the S. aureus accessions in SAURUS. Quality-controlled with one consistent pipeline and CC0-licensed: a great alternative when you want plug-and-play FASTAs for the same genomes without downloading raw reads.

🛠️ Retrieval tools

Feed your export file to one of these:

  • fastq-dl — One command per accession. .fastq.gz output.
  • NCBI SRA Toolkit — Official prefetch + fasterq-dump workflow with MD5 integrity checks.

Example: fastq-dl --accession SRR12282488 --provider sra --outdir ./fastqs

Alternative for uniformly processed assemblies (not raw reads): allthebacteria.org

⚡ SAURUS-CLI — for terminal users & pipelines

Same dataset, same filters — in your terminal. The complete database ships inside the pip, conda, and Docker install. No downloads, no API keys, no network access after install.

saurus-cli v0.4.0 NEW + SRA EXPORT

One command. One accession list. One pipeline step.

Lineage signatures, JSON config, multi-format output — everything you need to embed SAURUS queries in Nextflow, Snakemake, or a public health lab's quarterly reporting script. The full 134,160-genome dataset is bundled at install time — and 22,277 genomes export SRA Run / BioSample / BioProject accessions ready for fastq-dl and the SRA Toolkit.

$ pip install saurus-cli $ saurus --lineage USA300 --country USA --source blood --count 907 matching genomes # Assembly accessions → NCBI datasets $ saurus --lineage USA300 | datasets download genome accession --inputfile /dev/stdin # NEW: raw-read accessions → fastq-dl / prefetch $ saurus --lineage USA300 --format sra -o usa300_runs → 3,847 SRR/ERR/DRR accessions # NEW: BioSample / BioProject exports $ saurus --lineage USA100 --format biosample -o usa100_samples $ saurus --lineage USA100 --format bioproject -o usa100_projects
