# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

SAURUS and the ARGUS initiative are open scientific infrastructure. They
are maintained by a small team and used by researchers at every career
stage, in every region, and from many kinds of institutions. We want
contributions and discussions to be welcoming to anyone who brings
good faith, regardless of their background, institution, geography,
career stage, or prior experience with open-source software.

## Expected behaviour

- Be direct and be kind. Critical feedback is welcome. Personal attacks
  are not.
- Assume good faith. If a message reads ambiguously, ask before
  escalating.
- Credit others. When you use someone else's idea, code, dataset, or
  prior work, cite it.
- Respect scientific norms. Claims about genomes, lineages, or
  resistance determinants should be verifiable from the data or the
  cited literature.
- Keep disagreements about the work, not about the person.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussion.
- Discriminatory comments related to gender, gender identity, sexual
  orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race,
  ethnicity, nationality, religion, or career stage.
- Publishing private information (email addresses, institutional
  details, unpublished data) without explicit permission.
- Dismissing a contributor because of their institution, region, or
  career stage.
- Submitting someone else's data, code, or text as your own.

## Scope

This code applies to:

- Issues, pull requests, and discussions in any ARGUS repository
- Review comments and commit messages
- Communication through project email addresses
- Any space where someone is representing the project

## Enforcement

The maintainer will review each report and take one of the following
actions:

1. **No action** if the report does not describe a violation of this
   code.
2. **Private conversation** with the person whose behaviour was
   reported, without naming the reporter.
3. **Public correction** in the relevant thread if the behaviour was
   public and the correction serves the community.
4. **Temporary or permanent restriction** from participating in the
   project's spaces if the behaviour is repeated or severe.

Restrictions are a last resort. The goal of enforcement is to keep the
project usable, not to punish.
